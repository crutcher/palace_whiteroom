# eigsolve

The L3 (iteration-rotation) view of `eigsolve` — the **canonical opaque-library partial-obstruction** case. The per-step **body** — the named shift-invert spectral-transform application `apply_shift_invert = apply_linop(op.operand) ▷ ksp_solve(op.inv)` — lifts cleanly to a global tensor-field expression (it is whole-tensor by signature shape, and is **identity-in-form to the firm L2 [`eigsolve`](../L2/eigsolve.md) body**). **The eigen-iteration loop does not lift**: unlike L3 [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) (whose Palace-authored convergence-test loop renders as an explicit value-threaded tail recursion), `eigsolve` has **no Palace-authored loop to render** — the eigen-iteration (Krylov-Schur restart, Arnoldi/Lanczos basis extension, Rayleigh-Ritz extraction, convergence test) is entirely inside SLEPc `EPSSolve` / ARPACK `naupd` RCI. The loop is therefore a witnessed [`sequential-obstruction`](../concepts/sequential-obstruction.md) rooted in **opaque-library-ownership**, with no removable recurrence to rotate. Companion to L2 [`eigsolve`](../L2/eigsolve.md) (the same body as a primitive composition with the eigen-iteration view erased) and L1 [`eigsolve`](../L1/eigsolve.md) (the opaque eigensolver-as-operator collapse). The firm L4 cap is [`L4/eigsolve`](../L4/eigsolve.md) (cycle-048): this entry's body/loop split lowers from that cap by an **in-line marker-erasure** (the cap's `Solve`/`EigOutcome` wrapper erases; the cap marks the same opaque-library obstruction this entry marks — the loop is marked-not-rendered at both layers, so there is no dedicated L4>L3 theme). This entry's status is unchanged by the cap landing — the loop's opaque-library ownership is not affected.

## Context

L3 is the iteration-rotation layer: where the L2 algebra admits a global tensor-field form, L3 captures it; where no global form exists, the **obstruction** is a first-class output (per [`sequential-obstruction`](../concepts/sequential-obstruction.md)). `eigsolve` at L3 is the canonical **opaque-library partial-obstruction** case: the body lifts, the loop does not — and the loop's non-lifting reason is distinct from the precedent partial-obstruction operator.

Two precedent L3 operators frame this entry:

- L3 [`krylov-step`](./krylov-step.md) (firm) is the **kernel** half of a Palace-authored kernel/driver pair: its per-step body lifts (whole-tensor primitives), and its consuming outer loop (L3 [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md)) renders the Palace-authored convergence-test driver as an explicit `iterate_while_L3` tail recursion. The `(krylov-step, ksp_solve)` pair is the shape `eigsolve` would have **if Palace authored an eigen-iteration loop** — but it does not.
- L3 [`chebyshev`](./chebyshev.md) (`partial-obstruction`) is the canonical **body-lifts-loop-doesn't** case: its inner `k`-recurrence and outer `pc_it` Richardson sweep are sequential obstructions rooted in **numerical stability** (Phillips & Fischer 2022 §2 — the recurrence form is chosen over explicit polynomial expansion). `eigsolve` shares the partial-obstruction *shape* but its obstruction is rooted in **opaque-library-ownership**: there is no Palace-authored recurrence at all — the loop is inside SLEPc/ARPACK.

The load-bearing structural fact this entry records: **`eigsolve` at L3 has a lifting body and a non-lifting loop, and the loop's non-lift is because Palace authors no loop.** The eigen-iteration is reverse-communication-interface (RCI) at ARPACK — Palace's loop body is a callback dispatcher, not an algorithm — and PC-shell-callback at SLEPc — Palace supplies only a matvec. This is the cycle-021 `l3-eigsolve-linear-evp-has-no-krylov-step-kernel-analog` prediction enacted: the linear-EVP eigsolve lands `partial-obstruction`, the eigen-iteration is opaque-library-owned.

The relationship to the adjacent layers:

- **Upward** to L4: the firm L4 cap is [`L4/eigsolve`](../L4/eigsolve.md) (cycle-048) — the `Solve`-monadic outer-driver cap that consumes the [`solve-monad`](../concepts/solve-monad.md) outer-coordination surface (with a sum-typed `EigOutcome` termination richer than `ksp_solve`'s soft-fail — a first-class partial-success arm). Crucially, the cap is **not** a clean [`iterate_while`](../L4/iterate-while.md) fold: because the eigen-iteration loop is library-owned, the cap is a **role-naming obstruction-marker wrapper** (it names the eigen-iteration by role and marks the obstruction; it does not render a Palace loop, because Palace authors none). This entry's body/loop split lowers from that cap by an in-line marker-erasure (the L4>L3 relationship is recorded in-line in the cap's §"Lowers to" — no dedicated theme, parallel to `chebyshev`'s in-line-by-design L4>L3).
- **Downward** to L2: [`eigsolve`](../L2/eigsolve.md) (firm, cycle-023) is the same per-step body as a base-algebra primitive composition with the eigen-iteration view erased (the eigen-iteration fold referenced by role only — the L2 entry already names the fold opaque and opens only its body). The L3>L2 rotation on the **body** is **identity-in-form**: the L3 per-step tensor-field update `apply_shift_invert` maps line-for-line to the L2 `apply_shift_invert` composition (the L2 §Signature `apply_shift_invert` is exactly this body with the same `apply_linop ▷ ksp_solve ▷ scale_untransform` three-stage shape). The **substantive** part of the hop is the **loop**: L3 makes the (un-liftable, opaque-library-owned) eigen-iteration loop *explicit as an obstruction marker*, whereas L2 references the fold by role only — this marker-erasure is the third / **opaque-library** erasure-scope root. The L3>L2 theme [`L3-L2/eigsolve-opaque-eigen-iteration`](../L3-L2/eigsolve-opaque-eigen-iteration.md) (firm, cycle-045) narrates both halves: the body identity-in-form + the loop-marker erasure. (Distinct from the cycle-012 non-adjacent-identity convention, which governs only fully-identity hops — this hop is substantive on the loop, so it carries a theme file.)

## Signature

```text
eigsolve :: (E, control) -> EigResult
```

The boundary signature is identical to the firm L1 [`eigsolve`](../L1/eigsolve.md) / L2 [`eigsolve`](../L2/eigsolve.md) (the `EigSolver[problem]` / `EigControl` / `EigResult[N, K_max]` record shapes are defined at L1 and not re-specified — the L3↔L2 rotation is on the *body* and the *loop view*, not the boundary type). What L3 adds is the **iteration-rotation view**: the eigensolve is the opaque library eigen-iteration `eigen_iterate` folding the named per-step **shift-invert spectral-transform application** `apply_shift_invert` over a Krylov basis, with the body recorded as a lifting whole-tensor expression and the fold recorded as a non-lifting `sequential-obstruction`.

The per-step body L3 lifts (value-threaded positional form; L3 has no `readonly` annotation and no monadic effect):

```text
apply_shift_invert :: (op, v) -> v'

-- spectral-transformation = none (no transform):        apply M⁻¹ K  (or M⁻¹ alone, per backend)
-- spectral-transformation = shift-invert (linear):      (K − σM)⁻¹ M
-- spectral-transformation = shift-invert (quadratic):   (L₀ − σL₁)⁻¹ L₁   -- PEP block linearization

apply_shift_invert op v =
  let w  = apply_linop op.operand v        -- whole-tensor operator-apply: M (linear) / K (none) / PEP block L₁
  let y  = ksp_solve op.inv w              -- inner ksp_solve inverting the shifted operator (K − σM) (or (L₀ − σL₁))
  let y' = scale_untransform op y          -- the per-backend γ / δ un-scale (informational coordinate bookkeeping)
  in if op.has_projector then apply_linop op.projector y' else y'  -- optional divergence-free projection tail
```

Shape contract (positional values; named axes; the operator-domain shape group `S` and the square operator form `LinOp[(S: ...), $S]` follow the named-shape-group convention of [`l4_calculus`](../semantics/index.md) §1.2.1–§1.2.2 — `complex` is an element type, not an axis):

- **`op`** — operator-parameters value, closure-captured by the body (first positional argument, never in the return position). The body reads:
  - `op.operand : LinOp[(S: ...), $S]` — the square operator `apply_linop` is applied against *before* the inner solve. Linear shift-invert: the bound mass operator `M` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:579` `opM->Mult(x1, z1)`); no-transform: `K` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:573` `opK->Mult(x1, z1)`); quadratic PEP: the linearization block `L₁` assembled from `(C, M)`. Element type `ComplexOperator` (inherited from L1).
  - `op.inv : Solver[(K − σM)]` — the inner [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) value inverting the *shifted* operator `(K − σM)` (linear) or `(L₀ − σL₁)` (quadratic). The shift `σ` and spectral-transform mode (STSINVERT / STPRECOND) are bound into `op.inv`'s operator at construction (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:379-394`); the inner solve sees an already-shifted system operator. `op.inv = E.linear`, the L1 `E.linear` opened (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:191-194` / `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:364-367`, both `opInv = &ksp`).
  - `op.projector : LinOp[(S: ...), $S]` (optional) — the square divergence-free projector applied to the result when bound (`opProj->Mult(y1)`, `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:586` / `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1870`).
- **`v`** — `Tensor[(S: ...), complex]` — the input Krylov basis vector (congruent to the operator domain shape group `S`; `complex` is the element type) the eigen-iteration hands to the per-step application (ARPACK: `x1.Set(px, n, false)` host-pointer convention, `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:570`; SLEPc: `FromPetscVec(x, ctx->x1)`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1857`). Read-only.
- **result `v'`** — `Tensor[$S, complex]` — the transformed vector `(K − σM)⁻¹ M v`, returned to the eigen-iteration to extend the Krylov basis (ARPACK: `y1.Get(py, n, false)`, `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:589`; SLEPc: `ToPetscVec(ctx->y1, y)`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1873`). Same shape group `S`; value-threaded (no aliasing with `v`).

There is **no per-step `outputs` record** in the body signature: the eigen-iteration consumes the transformed vector directly and produces no Palace-visible per-step readout (contrast L3 [`krylov-step`](./krylov-step.md), whose `(K', s', outputs)` carries a demand-prunable readout — but `krylov-step`'s loop is Palace-authored, so its readout is observable; `eigsolve`'s loop is opaque, so there is no per-step Palace observation point). The eigen-iteration's only product is the converged eigenpair set, extracted post-loop.

L4 wrapper machinery absent at L3 (structural for the layer; same dissolution shape as `krylov-step` / `chebyshev`):

1. **No `Solve` monad.** The L4 cap [`L4/eigsolve`](../L4/eigsolve.md) (firm cycle-048) carries the `Solve`-monadic outer-coordination over `EigState`; at L3 it dissolves to explicit value-threading (the per-step body is already in the explicit positional form). The cap's `EigOutcome` sum dissolves to this entry's `count→EigStatus` fold-terminal readout; the cap's `eigen_iterate` obstruction marker is preserved (the marker-erasure leaves the obstruction marked at both layers).
2. **No `readonly` capability typing.** The variant-absorption discipline (the body does not branch on `op`'s spectral-transformation / problem-type / backend-orchestration fields) is a documented invariant verified by reading the body, not a typed annotation.
3. **No closure-typed variant absorption.** The per-backend assembly mechanism (ARPACK explicit `ApplyOp` vs SLEPc ST-shell `__pc_apply_EPS`) collapses to one positional `op` value; the body's textual shape does not branch on backend.

## Semantics

`eigsolve` at L3 is the eigenpair-finding computation expressed over the named shift-invert spectral-transform body, with the iteration view rendered as a **lifting body folded by a non-lifting opaque-library loop**. Three phases in dataflow order:

1. **Setup + spectral-transform binding.** Destructure `E` into the per-step operator surface `op` (the bound problem operators `K`, `M`, `[C, A2]`; the inner solver `E.linear`; the shift `σ` and spectral-transform mode) and seed the eigen-iteration state from `control.initial_space`. The shift-invert binding (`SetShiftInvert`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:379-394`) selects which operator the inner [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) inverts: STSINVERT binds the exact-inverse `(K − σM)⁻¹` action (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:388`), STPRECOND binds an approximate-inverse preconditioner-grade solve (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:384`, the **shift-invert-precond** variant). ARPACK's problem mode is set to `iparam[6] = sinvert ? 3 : 1` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:273`) — mode 3 is ARPACK's shift-invert mode. The target `EPSSetTarget(eps, sigma/gamma)` is deferred to solve time (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:674`); the `σ/γ` form is the scaled-coordinate target.

2. **The lifting per-step body folded by the non-lifting opaque library eigen-iteration.** This is the iteration-rotation core and the load-bearing partial-obstruction structure.

   **The body lifts.** Each fold step is exactly one `apply_shift_invert` invocation — `apply_linop(op.operand, v)`, then inner `ksp_solve(op.inv, ·)`, then the scale-un-transform, then the optional divergence-free projection. Every line is a global tensor-field expression: `apply_linop` is whole-tensor by signature shape, the inner `ksp_solve` is whole-tensor in / whole-tensor out (L3-native by the firm L3 [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) signature), the scale is a scalar multiply, the projection is one `apply_linop`. There is no per-element dependence within the body. It is witnessed identically at the ARPACK explicit site (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:562-590` — `opM->Mult(x1, z1); opInv->Mult(z1, y1); y1 *= gamma` then `opProj->Mult(y1)`) and the SLEPc ST-shell site (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1847-1876` for the inverse-apply `ctx->opInv->Mult(ctx->x1, ctx->y1)` at :1858, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1801-1827` for the A0/A1 original-operator shell matvecs). **The body is identity-in-form to the firm L2 [`eigsolve`](../L2/eigsolve.md) `apply_shift_invert` body** (same three-stage composition).

   **The loop does not lift.** The eigen-iteration *fold* — the Krylov-Schur restart logic, the Arnoldi/Lanczos basis extension, the Rayleigh-Ritz extraction, the convergence test — is **entirely library-owned**, with no Palace-authored recurrence to rotate:
   - **SLEPc**: the entire iteration is one opaque call `EPSSolve(eps)` (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:694`, inside `SlepcEPSSolverBase::Solve`, `:687-709`). Palace supplies only the PC-shell callback `__pc_apply_EPS` (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1847-1876`) and the A0/A1 shell matvecs; SLEPc's `STSINVERT` machinery (set up at `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:391` `STSetMatMode(st, ST_MATMODE_SHELL)`) composes them. There is no Palace loop at all — the iteration is a single library function call.
   - **ARPACK**: the iteration is a reverse-communication-interface (RCI) loop. Palace's `while(true)` loop (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:315-339`) calls the ARPACK driver `naupd` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:318`) and dispatches the per-step matvec `ApplyOp(&workd[ipntr[0]-1], &workd[ipntr[1]-1])` only when `naupd` returns the reverse-communication tag `ido == 1 || ido == -1` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:323-326`), breaking when `ido == 99` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:331-334`). **The loop body is a callback dispatcher, not an algorithm** — all eigen-iteration logic (basis extension, restart, convergence) is inside `naupd`. The `iparam[2] = arpack_it` max-iteration bound (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:270`) is an ARPACK parameter, not a Palace-rendered convergence predicate.

   Recorded as a [`sequential-obstruction`](../concepts/sequential-obstruction.md) rooted in **opaque-library-ownership**: there is no removable recurrence to rotate, because Palace authors no recurrence. This is distinct from the L3 [`krylov-step`](./krylov-step.md) outer-loop obstruction (where Palace authors the loop, renders it as `iterate_while_L3`, and the obstruction is the non-closed-form scalar dependence) and from the L3 [`chebyshev`](./chebyshev.md) obstruction (where Palace authors the recurrence, renders it as explicit tail recursion, and the obstruction is numerical-stability non-removability). For `eigsolve`, the rotation cannot even render the loop — there is no Palace loop to render.

3. **Eigenpair extraction.** The firm L1 readout: un-transform (the shift-invert returns `1/(λ − σ)`-space eigenvalues; `GetEigenvalue` undoes both the shift and the Higham scaling, returning original-problem `λ` — `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:713-716`, `return l * gamma`), normalize (`RescaleEigenvectors`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:707`), residual, and the count→`EigStatus` structural map. Referenced from L1, not re-derived at L3.

The result is determined by `(E, control)` modulo the four load-bearing non-determinism sources the firm L1 entry catalogs (reduction-tree non-associativity in the inner BLAS-1 ops, per-backend floating-point ordering, inner-`ksp_solve` non-determinism propagated to the outer eigensolve, library-internal RNG for initial-space generation). The L3 view inherits these through its lifting `apply_shift_invert` body (whose inner `ksp_solve` is itself non-deterministic) and through the opaque library loop (whose basis management and RNG are library-internal).

### Value-threaded form (L3 rendering)

```text
eigsolve E control =
  let (op, st0)  = setup E control                  -- bind K, M, [C, A2]; bind σ, mode; bind op.inv = E.linear; seed from control.initial_space
  let pairs      = eigen_iterate op st0 apply_shift_invert  -- OPAQUE library fold (SLEPc EPSSolve / ARPACK naupd RCI) — NOT renderable as iterate_while_L3
  in extract_eigpairs op pairs                       -- un-transform (l * gamma) + normalize + residual + count→status, per the firm L1 EigResult readout
  where
    apply_shift_invert op v =                        -- the per-step body that LIFTS (whole-tensor; identity-in-form to L2)
      let w  = apply_linop op.operand v
      let y  = ksp_solve op.inv w
      let y' = scale_untransform op y
      in if op.has_projector then apply_linop op.projector y' else y'
```

Unlike L3 [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) (whose body has the form `eigsolve ... = ... in itloop ...` with an explicit `iterate_while_L3` tail recursion rendering the Palace-authored convergence-test loop), `eigsolve`'s `eigen_iterate` is **not** renderable as a tail recursion — it is a single opaque library call (`EPSSolve`) or an RCI callback-dispatch loop (`naupd`). The `where`-bound `apply_shift_invert` is the only Palace-authored, L3-renderable piece; `eigen_iterate` is named by role only (the `sequential-obstruction`).

### Iteration-rotation marker

L3 is the iteration-rotation layer. `eigsolve`'s iteration view is the relationship between successive Krylov basis vectors `v_k -> v_{k+1} = apply_shift_invert(op, v_k)` (the per-step body) and the library-internal basis/restart management (the fold).

- **The body lifts.** Each per-step `apply_shift_invert` line is a global tensor-field expression (whole-tensor by signature shape). It is identity-in-form to the firm L2 [`eigsolve`](../L2/eigsolve.md) `apply_shift_invert` body. The spectral-transformation / problem-type / backend-orchestration variant branches lift trivially (they select `op.operand` and `op.inv`, not the body's textual shape).
- **The eigen-iteration loop does not lift** — it is opaque-library-owned. SLEPc folds the body inside `EPSSolve(eps)` (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:694`); ARPACK folds it inside the `naupd` RCI driver (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:318`, dispatched by the callback loop `:315-339`). There is **no Palace-authored eigen-step kernel / eigen-iteration driver pair** analogous to `(krylov-step, ksp_solve)` — the iteration is entirely library-internal, and the rotation cannot render a loop that Palace does not author. Recorded as a [`sequential-obstruction`](../concepts/sequential-obstruction.md) rooted in opaque-library-ownership.

This is the **opaque-library partial obstruction**: body lifts, loop does not, and the loop's non-lift is because Palace authors no loop (per [`tensor-field-lift`](../concepts/tensor-field-lift.md)). It is **identity-in-form to the L2 body** precisely because all sequentiality is in the surrounding (library-owned) loop, not in the body.

## Algebraic laws

The laws below hold; absences are deliberate. They are (a) **body-composition** laws on `apply_shift_invert` (syntactic identities read from the positive Palace `ApplyOp` / `__pc_apply_EPS` bodies — restated in L3 vocabulary from the firm L2 entry, where the body is identity-in-form) and (b) **fold-terminal** restatements of the firm L1 eigsolve laws (the eigenvalue defining equation, shift-invariance, normalization) as properties of the (library-owned) fold's converged fixed point. The obstruction structure is made explicit at L3.

1. **Shift-invert body composition identity** (exact; the load-bearing L3 body law). For the linear shift-invert case, the per-step body factors exactly as `apply_shift_invert(op, v) = scale_untransform(ksp_solve(op.inv, apply_linop(op.M, v)))`. This is a *syntactic identity* read directly from `ArpackEPSSolver::ApplyOp` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:579-581`: `opM->Mult(x1, z1); opInv->Mult(z1, y1); y1 *= gamma`) — the composition is `apply_linop(M) ▷ ksp_solve ▷ (·× γ)`, in that order, with the optional `opProj->Mult` tail at `:586`. The SLEPc path realizes the identical action through the ST-shell decomposition (`__pc_apply_EPS` inverse-apply `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1856-1865` composed with `__mat_apply_EPS_A0/A1` matvecs `:1801-1827`); the two assembly sites are extensionally equal. The no-transform case is the dual identity `apply_linop(K) ▷ ksp_solve(M⁻¹)` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:572-575`). Identity-in-form to firm L2 law 1.

2. **Per-step body whole-tensor lift.** Each line of `apply_shift_invert` is a global tensor-field expression: `apply_linop op.operand v` is whole-tensor (L3-native by signature shape), `ksp_solve op.inv w` is whole-tensor in / whole-tensor out (L3-native by the firm L3 [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) signature), `scale_untransform` is a scalar multiply, the optional `apply_linop op.projector` is whole-tensor. There is no per-element dependence within the body. **This is the body-half of the partial obstruction** — the law that the body lifts even though the surrounding loop does not.

3. **Spectral-transform eigenvalue correspondence** (modulo tolerance; the defining property the shift-invert buys). For each converged pair `(λᵢ, xᵢ)`, the *transformed* operator `(K − σM)⁻¹ M` has eigenvalue `θᵢ = 1/(λᵢ − σ)` at `xᵢ`, with `λᵢ` the original-problem eigenvalue. The (library-owned) eigen-iteration finds the dominant `θᵢ` (largest magnitude — ARPACK `which::largest_magnitude`, `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:278`), which correspond to `λᵢ` nearest the shift `σ`. The correspondence is undone at extraction: `GetEigenvalue` returns original-problem `λᵢ = l * gamma` (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:713-716`), not the transformed `θᵢ` the iteration sees. This restates firm L1 law 4 as a fold-terminal property: the iteration runs in `θ`-space, the boundary returns `λ`-space.

4. **Scaling-coordinate un-transform at the body and boundary** (modulo tolerance). The shell matvecs carry the Higham `δ` / `δγ` premultipliers (`__mat_apply_EPS_A0` `*= delta` `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1810`; `__mat_apply_EPS_A1` `*= delta * gamma` `:1825`; ARPACK `y1 *= gamma` `:581`), so the eigen-iteration runs in scaled coordinates; the body's `scale_untransform` tail (`__pc_apply_EPS` `*= 1.0/(delta*gamma)` no-transform `:1861` / `*= 1.0/delta` shift-invert `:1865`) and the un-scale-at-accessor (`GetEigenvalue` `l * gamma` `:715`) restore original coordinates. The returned `EigResult.eigenvalues` are in original-problem coordinates (firm L1 law 5). Identity-in-form to firm L2 law 3.

5. **Inner-solve linearity inherited by the per-step body** (modulo inner-solve tolerance). `apply_shift_invert` is linear in `v`: `apply_linop` is exactly linear and `ksp_solve` is linear-in-RHS modulo tolerance (firm L3 [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) inherited from L2 law 3). So the per-step body inherits linearity in the Krylov basis vector — the algebraic substrate the (opaque) library Arnoldi/Lanczos basis extension composes against. A *constituent* body law, not an `eigsolve`-level law (the eigenproblem is nonlinear in `λ`).

Laws that explicitly **do not** hold:

- **Eigen-iteration loop lift to a tensor-field op OR to an explicit `iterate_while_L3` tail recursion.** The eigen-iteration fold (basis extension, restart, Rayleigh-Ritz, convergence test) does **not** lift to a global tensor-field operation, **and unlike L3 [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) it does not even render as an explicit tail recursion** — because Palace authors no loop. SLEPc folds inside `EPSSolve(eps)` (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:694`); ARPACK folds inside `naupd` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:318`, callback-dispatched by `:315-339`). This is the **opaque-library `sequential-obstruction`** — the loop-half of the partial obstruction. (The load-bearing non-law that drives the `partial-obstruction` status.)
- **Per-step body observability.** Unlike L3 [`krylov-step`](./krylov-step.md) (whose `outputs` record carries a per-step demand-prunable readout the Palace-authored loop observes), `eigsolve` has no Palace-visible per-step observation point — the eigen-iteration consumes the transformed vector and produces no per-step Palace readout (the only Palace observation is post-loop, at `EPSGetConverged` / `RescaleEigenvectors`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:695, 707`). The demand-pruning law statable at `krylov-step` is not statable here.
- **Restart / fold-merge associativity.** The library eigen-iteration's Krylov-Schur restart re-seeds the basis; iterating two restart cycles is not iterating one double-length cycle. The L3 entry cannot assert restart-associativity because the restart is library-internal (inherited from L2; the same non-law `krylov-step` and `ksp_solve` carry for Palace-authored loops, here strengthened because Palace authors no loop at all).
- **Bit-determinism across backend-orchestration variants** (ARPACK explicit-callback vs SLEPc ST-shell). The two backends fold the *same* body at different floating-point orderings (ARPACK host-only `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:570` host-pointer convention; SLEPc device-capable); the mathematical eigenpairs match modulo tolerance, the bit-level realization differs. Inherited from firm L1/L2.
- **Bit-determinism across reduction-tree / initial-space variants.** Inherited through the `apply_linop` and inner `ksp_solve` constituents and the library RNG (firm L1 non-laws). The body sees the cumulative bit-noise.
- **Determinism of `K_max` truncation.** Increasing the requested mode count rebuilds the (library-internal) Krylov basis differently; the first `K` returned pairs are not preserved (firm L1 non-law). The L3 view cannot assert otherwise because the basis management is library-owned.
- **Eigenvalue ordering.** The returned eigenvalues are in the library's internal convergence order; any sort is a downstream concern (firm L1 non-law). Not a property of the L3 view.
