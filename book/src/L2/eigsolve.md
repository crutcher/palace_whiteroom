# eigsolve

The L2 **shift-invert spectral-transform composition** for the generalized eigenproblem: the named composition `apply_shift_invert ▷ ksp_solve` that the firm L1 [`eigsolve`](../L1/eigsolve.md) unfolds into. Where L1 collapses the entire eigensolve — the eigen-iteration loop, the per-step operator-application, the inner linear solve, all of it — into one opaque `eigsolve :: (E, control) -> EigResult` (the *eigensolver-as-operator* collapse), this L2 entry opens **the per-step operator-application half** of that opacity into the canonical Palace composition: a constructed-operator apply (`apply_linop` against the bound `M` / PEP-block operand) fed into an inner [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) that inverts the shifted operator `(K − σM)`. The eigen-iteration *loop* that folds this composition is opaque-library-owned (SLEPc EPS Krylov-Schur / ARPACK RCI Arnoldi) — it is **not** opened at L2 (and resists opening at L3 — see §"Lifts to / Lowers to"). This entry names the **fold body**, not the fold; that is the L2 layer's discipline and the load-bearing distinction from the [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) entry (whose fold *is* Palace-authored and so *is* named at L2).

## Context

L2 is the fusion-rotation layer: each operation is written as a composition of base algebraic primitives, with HPC/SIMD tricks unfolded back into the base algebras. `eigsolve` at L2 is the **named-composition** motif (per [`L2/index`](./index.md) §"Named compositions"), another named composition alongside [`orthogonalize`](./orthogonalize.md) (`project ▷ subtract`) and [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) (the outer-driver convergence-test fold). Unlike those two, `eigsolve` at L2 is a **partial-opening** named composition: it names the *per-step operator-application* composition that the library eigen-iteration consumes, while leaving the eigen-iteration loop itself opaque. This is the central structural fact of the entry and the reason the L3 backfill is predicted `partial-obstruction`.

The L2 composition is the **shift-invert spectral transformation**. The generalized eigenproblem `K x = λ M x` (linear) is hard to solve directly when the spectrum target is interior or clustered; the standard Krylov-eigensolver remedy is to iterate against a *spectrally-transformed* operator whose dominant eigenvalues correspond to the target eigenvalues of the original problem. Palace uses **shift-and-invert**: iterate against `(K − σM)⁻¹ M` (linear case), whose eigenvalues `1/(λ − σ)` are largest for `λ` nearest the shift `σ`. The transformed-operator application is the per-step body the eigen-iteration folds — and that application is a clean composition of an `apply_linop` (against `M`, or the PEP/NEP operand) and an inner [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) (inverting the shifted operator `(K − σM)`). The shift-invert *setup* binds this composition: `SetShiftInvert(σ, precond)` (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:379-394`) selects the spectral-transform mode (STSINVERT exact-inverse vs STPRECOND approximate-inverse) and defers the target `EPSSetTarget(eps, σ/γ)` to solve time (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:671-685`); `SetLinearSolver(ksp)` binds the inner solver that performs the inversion (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:364-367` / `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:191-194`, both `opInv = &ksp`).

The two backends assemble the **identical L2 composition** at two distinct sites:

- **ARPACK** (`ArpackEPSSolver::ApplyOp`, `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:562-590`) makes the composition **explicit and Palace-owned**. The shift-invert branch is, line-for-line, `opM->Mult(x1, z1); opInv->Mult(z1, y1); y1 *= γ` — that is `apply_linop(M, x)` then the inner-`ksp_solve`-backed `apply_linop(opInv, ·)` then the scaling un-transform. This is the cleanest L2-expressible witness of the composition: `(K − σM)⁻¹ M x = apply_shift_invert(M, opInv, x)` where `apply_shift_invert = apply_linop(M) ▷ ksp_solve`.
- **SLEPc** (`__pc_apply_EPS`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1847-1877`) routes the *same action* `y = (K − σM)⁻¹ x` through the PETSc `ST` shell preconditioner: `ctx->opInv->Mult(ctx->x1, ctx->y1)` (the inner `ksp_solve`) followed by the `δ`/`δγ` un-scale. The original-problem operators surface as the outer shell matvecs `__mat_apply_EPS_A0` = `δ · opK·x` and `__mat_apply_EPS_A1` = `δγ · opM·x` (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1801-1827`); SLEPc's `STSINVERT` machinery composes the `(A0, A1)` matvecs with the `__pc_apply_EPS` inverse-apply to realize `(K − σM)⁻¹ M`. The composition is the same; ARPACK assembles it by hand, SLEPc delegates the assembly to the PETSc `ST` layer (`STSetMatMode(st, ST_MATMODE_SHELL)` at `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:391`).

The L2 entry names the composition that both sites realize; the per-backend assembly mechanism (explicit-hand-assembly vs PETSc-ST-delegated) is the **backend-orchestration** variant axis (collapsed at L2 to the single named composition, the way [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) collapses CG / GMRES nesting to one driver).

### Relationship to the L1 collapse

[`L1/eigsolve`](../L1/eigsolve.md) collapses the entire eigensolve — eigen-iteration loop, per-step transformed-operator application, inner linear solve, result extraction — into an **opaque** `eigsolve :: (E: EigSolver[problem], control: EigControl) -> EigResult`. That collapse is the *constructed-operator absorption* the L1 entry shares with [`ksp_solve`](../L1/ksp_solve.md): at L1 an eigensolve is one indivisible operator application, with `E` a black box that maps a problem to its eigenpairs. **This L2 entry opens the per-step-application half of that black box** into the shift-invert composition: the L1 opaque `E.linear : Solver[A]` becomes the explicit inner [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) inverting `(K − σM)`; the L1 absorbed *spectral-transformation* axis re-surfaces at L2 as the visible choice of which operator the inner solve inverts and what `apply_linop` operand feeds it; the L1 absorbed *backend-orchestration* axis re-surfaces as the explicit-vs-ST-delegated assembly site. **The L2 entry does not open the eigen-iteration loop** — that stays opaque (it is library-owned: SLEPc `EPSSolve` / ARPACK `naupd` RCI), the second half of the L1 opacity. This is the load-bearing contrast with [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md), whose loop *is* Palace-authored (`iterative.cpp`) and so is opened at L2 as the convergence-test fold.

The L2↔L1 rotation is **non-identity**: it is the *partial un-collapse* of the L1 opacity into a composition (the per-step body), leaving the loop collapsed. The L1 algebraic laws — eigenvalue defining equation, shift-invariance of returned eigenvalues, scaling-coordinate convention — restate at L2 as properties of the composition's fixed point and of the un-transform applied at the composition boundary (see §"Algebraic laws").

## Signature

```text
eigsolve :: (E: EigSolver[problem], control: EigControl) -> EigResult[N, K_max]
```

The boundary signature is identical to the firm L1 [`eigsolve`](../L1/eigsolve.md) (the `EigSolver[problem]` / `EigControl` / `EigResult[N, K_max]` record shapes are defined there and not re-specified — the L2↔L1 rotation is on the *body*, not the boundary type). What L2 adds is the **body composition**: the eigensolve is the opaque library eigen-iteration `eigen_iterate` folding the named **shift-invert spectral-transform application** `apply_shift_invert` over a Krylov basis. In L2 vocabulary, the body is:

```text
eigsolve E control =
  let (op, st)   = setup E control                       -- bind problem operators (K, M, [C, A2]); bind spectral-transform σ, mode; bind inner solver E.linear; seed basis from control.initial_space
  let pairs      = eigen_iterate op st apply_shift_invert  -- the OPAQUE library eigen-iteration (SLEPc EPS / ARPACK RCI) folding the per-step application — NOT a Palace-authored fold, NOT opened at L2
  in extract_eigpairs op pairs                            -- un-transform (shift / scaling) + normalize + residual + count→status, all per the firm L1 EigResult readout
```

where the named per-step composition `apply_shift_invert` is the L2 content this entry firms:

```text
apply_shift_invert :: (op: SpectralTransformOp, v: Tensor[N, complex]) -> Tensor[N, complex]

-- spectral-transformation = none (no transform):        apply M⁻¹ K  (or M⁻¹ alone, per backend)
-- spectral-transformation = shift-invert (linear):      (K − σM)⁻¹ M
-- spectral-transformation = shift-invert (quadratic):   (L₀ − σL₁)⁻¹ L₁   -- PEP block linearization

apply_shift_invert op v =
  let w  = apply_linop op.operand v        -- apply_linop against M (linear) / the PEP block L₁ operand (quadratic)
  let y  = ksp_solve op.inv w              -- inner ksp_solve inverting the shifted operator (K − σM) (or the PEP (L₀ − σL₁))
  in scale_untransform op y                -- the per-backend γ / δ un-scale (informational coordinate bookkeeping)
```

Shape contract (bunsen-style; named axes):

- **`op.operand`** — `LinearOperator[N, N]` — the operator `apply_linop` is applied against *before* the inner solve. For the linear shift-invert case it is the bound mass operator `M` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:579` `opM->Mult(x1, z1)`); for the no-transform case it is `K` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:573` `opK->Mult(x1, z1)`); for the quadratic PEP case it is the linearization block `L₁` assembled from `(C, M)` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:733-799`). The element type is `ComplexOperator` (the operator axis `N` matches the eigenproblem's square axis, inherited from L1).
- **`op.inv`** — `Solver[(K − σM)]` — the inner [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) value inverting the *shifted* operator `(K − σM)` (linear) or `(L₀ − σL₁)` (quadratic). This is the L1 `E.linear` opened: `op.inv = E.linear`, the construction-bound `ComplexKspSolver` (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:364-367` `opInv = &ksp`, wired at `palace/models/modeeigensolver.cpp:1037, 1050`). The shift `σ` and the spectral-transform mode (STSINVERT / STPRECOND) are bound into `op.inv`'s operator at construction (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:379-394`); the inner solve sees an already-shifted system operator.
- **`v`** — `Tensor[N, complex]` — the input Krylov basis vector the eigen-iteration hands to the per-step application (ARPACK: `x1.Set(px, n, false)` host-pointer convention, `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:570`; SLEPc: `FromPetscVec(x, ctx->x1)`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1858`). Read-only.
- **result of `apply_shift_invert`** — `Tensor[N, complex]` — the transformed vector `(K − σM)⁻¹ M v`, returned to the eigen-iteration to extend the Krylov basis (ARPACK: `y1.Get(py, n, false)`; SLEPc: `ToPetscVec(ctx->y1, y)`). When a divergence-free projector `E.projector` is bound, it is applied to the result (`opProj->Mult(y1)`, `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:586` / `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1870`) — a per-step constraint enforcement that is part of the named composition's tail.

The `eigen_iterate` fold and `extract_eigpairs` readout are **named by role, not opened** at L2 (the same role-reference discipline [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) §Semantics applies to its fold — except here the fold body `apply_shift_invert` IS Palace-authored and opened, while the fold itself is library-owned and stays a role reference). `extract_eigpairs` is the firm L1 readout (normalize via `RescaleEigenvectors`, residual via `GetResidualNorm`, shift/scaling un-transform at `GetEigenvalue`, count→status); it is referenced here, not re-derived.

## Semantics

`eigsolve` at L2 is the eigenpair-finding composition expressed over the named shift-invert spectral-transform application. The composition has three phases in dataflow order:

1. **Setup + spectral-transform binding** (`setup`). Destructure `E` into the per-step operator surface `op` (the bound problem operators `K`, `M`, `[C, A2]`; the inner solver `E.linear`; the shift `σ` and spectral-transform mode) and seed the eigen-iteration state from `control.initial_space`. The shift-invert binding (`SetShiftInvert`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:379-394`) selects which operator the inner [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) inverts: STSINVERT binds the exact-inverse `(K − σM)⁻¹` action, STPRECOND binds an approximate-inverse (the inner solve is a preconditioner-grade solve, the **shift-invert-precond** variant). The target `EPSSetTarget(eps, σ/γ)` is deferred to solve time (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:671-685`); the `σ/γ` form is the scaled-coordinate target (the un-scale-at-accessor convention restores original coordinates on extraction, per L1 law 5).

2. **The opaque library eigen-iteration folding the named composition** (`eigen_iterate op st apply_shift_invert`). This is the body half L2 opens and the loop half L2 leaves opaque. **Opened (named, firm):** each fold step is exactly one `apply_shift_invert` invocation — `apply_linop(op.operand, v)` then inner `ksp_solve(op.inv, ·)` then the scale-un-transform — producing the next Krylov basis vector. The composition is the canonical Palace shift-invert pattern, witnessed identically at the ARPACK explicit site (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:562-590`) and the SLEPc ST-shell site (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1847-1877` for the inverse-apply, `:1801-1827` for the original-operator matvecs). **Opaque (role reference, NOT opened):** the eigen-iteration *loop* — the Krylov-Schur restart logic, the Arnoldi/Lanczos basis extension, the Rayleigh-Ritz extraction, the convergence test — is library-owned (SLEPc `EPSSolve(eps)`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:694`; ARPACK `naupd`/`neupd` RCI driver, `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:263-358`). Palace authors **no** eigen-iteration kernel/driver pair analogous to the `(krylov-step, ksp_solve)` pair — the iteration is entirely inside SLEPc/ARPACK. L2 therefore names the fold by role and opens only its per-step body.

3. **Eigenpair extraction** (`extract_eigpairs`). The firm L1 readout: un-transform (the shift-invert returns `1/(λ − σ)`-space eigenvalues; `GetEigenvalue` undoes both the shift and the Higham scaling, returning original-problem `λ`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:711-716`), normalize (`RescaleEigenvectors` → unit-`‖·‖₂` or B-orthonormal), residual (`GetResidualNorm` builds `‖(K − λM)x‖₂`), and the count→`EigStatus` structural map. Referenced from L1, not re-derived at L2.

The **central structural fact**: `eigsolve` at L2 is a named composition whose **fold body is Palace-authored (and opened)** but whose **fold is library-owned (and not opened)**. This is the inverse decomposition from [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) at L2, where the fold IS opened (the convergence-test driver is Palace-authored in `iterative.cpp`) and the fold body is the companion [`krylov-step`](./krylov-step.md) kernel. For `eigsolve` there is no companion "eigen-step kernel + eigen-iteration driver" pair to author — the iteration is opaque-library, so only the per-step shift-invert *application* is an L2 composition. This is the load-bearing reason the L3 backfill (the iteration-rotation view) is predicted `partial-obstruction`: the per-step body lifts (it is the documented `apply_linop ▷ ksp_solve` composition), but the eigen-iteration loop does not lift to a global tensor-field expression — there is no Palace-authored loop to rotate, and the library loop is opaque.

The result is determined by `(E, control)` modulo the four load-bearing non-determinism sources the firm L1 entry catalogs (reduction-tree non-associativity in the inner BLAS-1 ops, per-backend floating-point ordering, inner-`ksp_solve` non-determinism propagated to the outer eigensolve, and library-internal RNG for initial-space generation). These are not re-derived here; the L2 composition inherits them through its `apply_linop` and inner `ksp_solve` constituents (the inner `ksp_solve` is itself non-deterministic per [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md), and that non-determinism propagates to the per-step transformed-operator application and hence to the Krylov basis).

The **divergence-free projection** is part of the named composition's tail when bound: `op.projector->Mult(result)` is applied after the inner solve at both backends (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:586`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1870`). It enforces the `∇·E = 0` constraint per step inside the eigen-iteration; at L2 it is a constituent of `apply_shift_invert`, not a separate composition.

## Algebraic laws

`eigsolve` at L2 is a **named composition** (a per-step spectral-transform application folded by an opaque library iteration), not a binary algebra. The laws below are (a) **composition-identity** laws on `apply_shift_invert` (syntactic identities read from the positive Palace `ApplyOp` / `__pc_apply_EPS` bodies — these are the L2 content this entry firms) and (b) **fold-terminal** restatements of the firm L1 eigsolve laws (the eigenvalue defining equation, shift-invariance, normalization) as properties of the composition's converged fixed point. The (a) laws stand on positive source; the (b) laws inherit firm status from the firm L1 entry. This is the same firm basis as [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) at L2 (composition-structural laws on positive `Mult` bodies + fold-terminal restatements of L1 laws).

1. **Shift-invert composition identity** (exact; the load-bearing L2 composition law). For the linear shift-invert case, the per-step transformed application factors exactly as `apply_shift_invert(op, v) = scale_untransform(ksp_solve(op.inv, apply_linop(op.M, v)))`. This is a *syntactic identity* read directly from `ArpackEPSSolver::ApplyOp` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:579-581`: `opM->Mult(x1, z1); opInv->Mult(z1, y1); y1 *= γ`) — the composition is `apply_linop(M) ▷ ksp_solve ▷ (·× γ)`, in that order. The SLEPc path realizes the identical action through the ST-shell decomposition (`__pc_apply_EPS` inverse-apply `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1858-1865` composed with `__mat_apply_EPS_A0/A1` matvecs `:1801-1827`); the two assembly sites are extensionally equal (`(K − σM)⁻¹ M v` either way). The no-transform case is the dual identity `apply_linop(K) ▷ ksp_solve(M⁻¹)` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:573-575`).

2. **Spectral-transform eigenvalue correspondence** (modulo tolerance; the defining property the shift-invert buys). For each converged pair `(λᵢ, xᵢ)`, the *transformed* operator `(K − σM)⁻¹ M` has eigenvalue `θᵢ = 1/(λᵢ − σ)` at `xᵢ`, with `λᵢ` the original-problem eigenvalue. The eigen-iteration finds the dominant `θᵢ` (largest magnitude), which correspond to `λᵢ` nearest the shift `σ` — this is the whole point of the spectral transform. The correspondence is undone at extraction: `GetEigenvalue` returns original-problem `λᵢ` (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:711-716`), not the transformed `θᵢ` the iteration sees. This restates firm L1 law 4 (eigenvalue invariance under shift) as a property of the L2 composition: the composition iterates in `θ`-space, the boundary returns `λ`-space.

3. **Scaling-coordinate un-transform at the composition boundary** (modulo tolerance, for the polynomial / nonlinear cases). The shell matvecs carry the Higham `δ` / `δγ` premultipliers (`__mat_apply_EPS_A0` `*= δ`, `__mat_apply_EPS_A1` `*= δγ`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1810, 1825`; ARPACK `y1 *= γ`, `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:581`), so the eigen-iteration runs in scaled coordinates; the composition boundary un-scales (`GetEigenvalue` returns `l * γ`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:715`). The returned `EigResult.eigenvalues` are in original-problem coordinates uniformly across backends (firm L1 law 5, re-verified by the cycle-012 4-backend audit). At L2 this is the `scale_untransform` tail of `apply_shift_invert` plus the un-scale-at-accessor in `extract_eigpairs` — the composition's coordinate bookkeeping.

4. **Inner-solve linearity inherited by the per-step application** (modulo inner-solve tolerance). `apply_shift_invert` is linear in `v` (its input basis vector): `apply_linop` is exactly linear and `ksp_solve` is linear-in-RHS modulo tolerance (firm L2 [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) law 3 — only the terminal solution `x` is linear, not the statistics). So the per-step transformed application inherits linearity in the Krylov basis vector. This is the algebraic substrate the (opaque) Arnoldi/Lanczos basis extension composes against — it is what makes the eigen-iteration a Krylov method at all. It is a *constituent* law, not an `eigsolve`-level law (the eigenproblem is nonlinear in `λ`).

Laws that explicitly **do not** hold:

- **Opening of the eigen-iteration fold at L2**. Unlike [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) (whose fold is the Palace-authored convergence-test driver, opened at L2), `eigsolve`'s fold is the opaque library eigen-iteration (SLEPc `EPSSolve` / ARPACK RCI). It is **not** opened at L2 — there is no Palace-authored eigen-step kernel / eigen-iteration driver pair. The L2 entry names the fold by role only. (This is the load-bearing non-law that drives the L3 `partial-obstruction` prediction.)
- **Fold-merge / restart associativity**. The library eigen-iteration's Krylov-Schur restart re-seeds the basis; iterating two restart cycles is not iterating one double-length cycle (inherited from the underlying Krylov structure, the same non-law [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) and [`krylov-step`](./krylov-step.md) carry). The L2 entry cannot assert restart-associativity because the restart is library-internal.
- **Bit-determinism across backend-orchestration variants** (ARPACK explicit-assembly vs SLEPc ST-shell-delegated). The two backends assemble the *same* composition but at different floating-point orderings (ARPACK host-only `palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:570`; SLEPc device-capable); the mathematical eigenpairs match modulo tolerance, the bit-level realization differs. Inherited from firm L1.
- **Bit-determinism across reduction-tree / initial-space variants**. Inherited through the `apply_linop` and inner `ksp_solve` constituents and the library RNG (firm L1 non-laws). The composition sees the cumulative bit-noise.
- **Determinism of `K_max` truncation**. Increasing the requested mode count rebuilds the (library-internal) Krylov basis differently; the first `K` returned pairs are not preserved (firm L1 non-law). The L2 composition cannot assert otherwise because the basis management is library-owned.
- **Eigenvalue ordering**. The returned eigenvalues are in the library's internal convergence order; any sort is a downstream concern (firm L1 non-law; `modeeigensolver.cpp:484-492` re-sort). Not a property of the L2 composition.

## Dependencies

**Same-layer (L2)**:

- [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) — the inner linear solver inverting the shifted operator `(K − σM)`. **Direct, load-bearing dependency**: `apply_shift_invert`'s middle stage is `ksp_solve(op.inv, ·)`. This makes `eigsolve` the **first L2 named composition whose direct constituent is itself a constructed-solver composition** (the firm L2 outer-driver `ksp_solve`), composing two layers of solver-as-operator — the L2 statement of the L1 observation that `eigsolve` is "the first L1 operator to compose two layers of constructed-operator absorption". The inner `ksp_solve` is invoked once per eigen-iteration step at every `opInv->Mult` call site (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:574, 580, 761, 778`; `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1858`).
- [`apply_BA`](../concepts/apply_BA.md) — the constructed-operator framing: the inner solve inverts the *shifted constructed* operator `(K − σM)`, assembled from the bound `K`, `M`, and shift `σ`. The shift-invert operand assembly is a constructed-operator instance.

**Cross-layer constituents** (used by the named composition, defined at lower layers):

- [`apply_linop`](../L1/apply_linop.md) (L1, firm) — the first stage of `apply_shift_invert`: the operator-application against `op.operand` (`M` for linear shift-invert; the PEP block `L₁` for quadratic). Direct constituent. Witnessed `opM->Mult(x1, z1)` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:579`) / the `__mat_apply_EPS_A1` shell matvec (`palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:1817-1825`).
- [`dot`](../L1/dot.md), [`nrm2`](../L1/nrm2.md), [`axpy`](../L1/axpy.md), [`axpby`](../L1/axpby.md) (L1, firm) — transitively present in the library eigen-iteration (orthogonalization, residual norms, basis updates) and in the inner `ksp_solve`; recorded as transitive because they appear inside the opaque eigen-iteration the L2 form names by role and inside the inner solver.

**Cross-cutting concepts** (consumed unchanged):

- [`constructed-operators`](../concepts/constructed-operators.md) — the shifted operator `(K − σM)` the inner solve inverts is a constructed operator; the spectral-transform binding is its construction.

## Variant axes

`eigsolve` at L2 has three opened variant axes (visible at the composition surface) and two collapsed axes (absorbed into the named composition).

Opened (shape the named composition):

1. **spectral-transformation** (`none | shift-invert | shift-invert-precond`) — selects which operator the inner `ksp_solve` inverts and what `apply_linop` operand feeds it. `none`: `apply_linop(K) ▷ ksp_solve(M⁻¹)` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:573-575`). `shift-invert` (STSINVERT, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:388`): `apply_linop(M) ▷ ksp_solve((K − σM)⁻¹)` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:579-581`). `shift-invert-precond` (STPRECOND, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:384`): the inner solve is preconditioner-grade (approximate inverse). This is the L1 absorbed spectral-transformation axis re-surfaced at composition granularity.
2. **problem-type** (`linear | quadratic | nonlinear`) — selects the operand-assembly the inner solve inverts. Linear: `(K − σM)`. Quadratic (PEP): the block linearization `(L₀ − σL₁)` with `L₀ = [[−K, 0], [0, M]]`, `L₁ = [[C, M], [M, 0]]`, the composition `(L₀ − σL₁)⁻¹ L₁` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:733-799`). Nonlinear (NEP): the per-`λ` operator `A(λ) = K + λC + λ²M + A2(λ)` (the [`apply_nonlinear_pencil`](../L1/apply_nonlinear_pencil.md) operand). The problem-type tag is part of `EigSolver[problem]`'s phantom (inherited from L1).
3. **scaling** (`NONE | NORM_2`) — the Higham `δ` / `γ` premultipliers on the shell matvecs and the un-scale tail of `apply_shift_invert`; informational at the result boundary (firm L1 law 5). Shapes the composition's coordinate bookkeeping, not its structure.

Collapsed (absorbed into the named composition):

- **backend-orchestration** (`arpack-rci | slepc-st-shell`) — the same `apply_linop ▷ ksp_solve` composition assembled two ways: ARPACK by explicit hand-assembly in `ApplyOp` (`palace/linalg/arpack.cpp:562-590`), SLEPc by delegation to the PETSc `ST` shell (`STSetMatMode(st, ST_MATMODE_SHELL)`, `palace/linalg/slepc.cpp:391`; the `__pc_apply_EPS` + `__mat_apply_EPS_A0/A1` triple). Collapsed to the single named composition at L2, the way [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) collapses solver-method nesting. The library eigen-iteration loop (also part of this orchestration) stays a role reference (the non-opening above).
- **element-type** (`complex only`) — Palace's `EigenvalueSolver` surface is complex-only (firm L1; `SetOperators` overloads all take `ComplexOperator`). No real-element variant.

## Lifts to

L2 `eigsolve` lifts to L3 [`eigsolve`](../L3/eigsolve.md) (currently a `stub` — predicted `partial-obstruction`; the L3 entry is materialized as a claim-free placeholder pending the cycle-024 L3 backfill that refines it in place): the L2 named-composition body (`apply_shift_invert`) lifts cleanly to the iteration-rotation layer — it is the `apply_linop ▷ ksp_solve` composition, which has a clean L3 per-step form. **But the eigen-iteration loop does not lift.** L2 names the eigen-iteration fold by role (it is opaque-library-owned); L3 would attempt to render it as an explicit value-threaded iteration and name its `sequential-obstruction` — but unlike [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) (whose L3 lift renders the Palace-authored convergence-test loop as an explicit tail recursion), `eigsolve` has **no Palace-authored loop to render** — the eigen-iteration is entirely inside SLEPc/ARPACK. ## L2 vs L1 distinction

- **L1**: opaque eigensolver-as-operator. `eigsolve :: (E, control) -> EigResult`. The eigen-iteration loop, the per-step transformed-operator application, the inner linear solve, the result extraction — all invisible; an eigensolve is one indivisible operator application. Spectral-transformation / backend-orchestration / scaling axes absorbed into the opaque `EigSolver[problem]`. Algebraic laws are fixed-point properties stated directly on the operator.
- **L2**: named shift-invert composition over the per-step application. `eigsolve :: (E, control) -> EigResult` with the per-step body opened to `apply_shift_invert = apply_linop ▷ ksp_solve` and the eigen-iteration fold named by role. The L1 spectral-transformation / backend-orchestration axes re-surface as opened/collapsed composition axes. The L1 fixed-point laws restate as composition-identity (on positive `ApplyOp` source) + fold-terminal laws. **The eigen-iteration loop stays erased** (role reference) — the load-bearing distinction from [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md), whose loop IS opened at L2.
