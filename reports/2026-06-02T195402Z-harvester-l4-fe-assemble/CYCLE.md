# fe_assemble

The L4 **assemble-fold combinator**: capture the finite-element space once, fold an immutable list of weak-form terms by the opaque per-term assembly leaf `assemble_term`, and **sum** the per-term contributions into a global linear operator `K`. Where [`solve_family`](./solve_family.md) is a `map` over an independent RHS family that *collects* a solution list, `fe_assemble` is a `map`-then-**reduce** over a weak-form-term list that *sums* into a single operator — it is the **concatenation-homomorphism `foldr` producing a sum**, the **homomorphic sibling** of `solve_family`'s map (the homomorphism HOLDS — see §Algebraic laws), distinct from [`fold_solve`](./fold_solve.md)'s carry-threaded sequential fold (whose homomorphism does NOT hold). The opaque per-term libCEED quadrature leaf `assemble_term` rises as a **black-box-kernel `readonly` input** — the same opaque-leaf-wrapping pattern [`eigsolve`](./eigsolve.md) and [`fold_solve`](./fold_solve.md) use for `eigen_iterate` / `time_step_op`.

## Context

L4's job is to write algorithms in a graph-evaluation calculus that makes lifetimes, dispatch sites, and effect placement structural (`L4/index.md:7-13`). `fe_assemble` is the **assemble-half** of the deliverable's L4 surface — the operator-construction verb every solver pipeline (electrostatic / magnetostatic / eigenmode / driven / transient) calls to build its system operator `K` before the [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) / [`eigsolve`](./eigsolve.md) / [`fold_solve`](./fold_solve.md) coordination shells drive it to a result. It is the genuinely-new L4 calculus vocabulary the FE cohort needed (directive 1: L4 is the outward backend-lowering target — the backend has its own assembly engine and wants a clean combinator surface, not the unfolded loop).

The combinator names the same fold the firm L1 [`fe_assemble`](../L1/fe_assemble.md) names (`K = Σ_i A(space, term_i)`), at the L4 calculus level — with three things made *structural* that are merely conventional at L1:

1. **The space is captured once, `readonly`.** `space : FiniteElementSpace[N]` is bound once, outside the term fold, and threaded *unchanged* into every per-term `assemble_term` — the L4 typing of the single `BilinearForm k(GetH1Space())` construction the term-appends share (`palace/models/laplaceoperator.cpp:193`). Per [`state-stratification`](../concepts/state-stratification.md), `space` is the shared `readonly` construction stratum; the term list is the per-call input.
2. **The per-term assembly leaf is an opaque black-box-kernel input.** `assemble_term :: FiniteElementSpace[N] -> WeakFormTerm -> LinearOperator[N,N]` is the element-local→global quadrature contraction (restriction + basis-apply + quadrature) — **libCEED-owned**, not Palace-authored. Per [`black-box-vs-accelerated-kernels`](../concepts/black-box-vs-accelerated-kernels.md) case 1, it rises as an **opaque-surface input** to the combinator: clean signature, opaque body, the external backend supplies the kernel. This is the IDENTICAL pattern [`eigsolve`](./eigsolve.md) (`eigen_iterate`) and [`fold_solve`](./fold_solve.md) (`time_step_op`) use; the combinator folds over `assemble_term` *without cracking it open*.
3. **The term list is a first-class `[WeakFormTerm]`.** The L0 two-list (domain + boundary) structure is one concatenated L1/L4 term list (`domain_integs ++ boundary_integs`, `palace/fem/bilinearform.cpp:71-98`); the boundary-vs-domain split is the absorbed `term-position` axis (the boundary terms assemble on dimension−1 geometry but enter the same accumulating sum).

The combinator is defined **in L4 vocabulary** (high→low discipline, CLAUDE.md §Methodology invariants): its semantics, signature, and laws are stated in terms of the `foldr`/sum combinator, the `[WeakFormTerm]` list, the `assemble_term` black-box leaf, and the [`state-stratification`](../concepts/state-stratification.md) `readonly` space stratum — NOT in terms of L3 value-threading primitives. The L4>L3 dissolution (the `foldr`/sum collapsing to the L3 global tensor-field assembly view — the explicit composite-operator accumulation with the per-term leaf bottoming out in the libCEED boundary) is the substantive [`fe-assemble-fold-dissolution`](../L4-L3/fe-assemble-fold-dissolution.md) theme, narrated forward L4→L3; it is **not** authored here (D2 of this cycle).

`fe_assemble` at L4 is a **methodology-level combinator** distilled from the FE-assembly machinery Palace shares across all five pipelines, not a Palace-source artefact per se — there is no single L0 range that "is" the L4 `fe_assemble`. The Palace evidence is the firm L1 fold + the integrator-fold L0 home (`palace/fem/bilinearform.cpp:28-107`) + the three witness term-construction sites (§Mining-gate); L4 names the `foldr`/sum combinator and the space-capture-once `readonly` stratification those sites share.

## Signature

The combinator captures the space once, outside the fold, and threads it unchanged into every per-term `assemble_term`; the per-term contributions are summed (the `foldr` reduces by operator-`+`):

    -- entry point: capture the space once, fold the term list by assemble_term, sum the contributions
    fe_assemble :: FiniteElementSpace[N] -> [WeakFormTerm] -> LinearOperator[N, N]
    fe_assemble space terms = foldr (\t acc -> assemble_term space t + acc) zero terms

    -- equivalently, as the map-then-reduce the foldr-over-a-commutative-monoid IS
    -- (each term independent; no carry; the reduction is operator-+ over LinearOperator[N,N]):
    fe_assemble space terms = sum [ assemble_term space t | t <- terms ]
                            = Σ_{t ∈ terms} assemble_term space t

    -- the opaque per-term assembly leaf: ONE weak-form term to its global-dof contribution.
    -- BLACK-BOX KERNEL (libCEED-owned element-local quadrature + restriction); rises as a
    -- readonly opaque-surface INPUT to the combinator, folded over WITHOUT cracking it open.
    assemble_term :: FiniteElementSpace[N] -> WeakFormTerm -> LinearOperator[N, N]

Shape contract (bunsen-style; named records and axes; the construction stratum per [`state-stratification`](../concepts/state-stratification.md)):

- `space` — `FiniteElementSpace[N]` — the trial/test finite-element space, **captured once at assembly construction; `readonly`** per [`state-stratification`](../concepts/state-stratification.md). `N = space.GetTrueVSize()` is the global true-dof count (the operator's square dimension). This is the load-bearing typing: `space` is bound *once, outside the fold*, and threaded *unchanged* into every `assemble_term` call — the L4 typing of the single `BilinearForm k(GetH1Space())` construction the term-appends share. The **three FE construction inputs** (`fe_space` / `fe_collection` / `essential_dofs`) are **absorbed into this `FiniteElementSpace[N]` `readonly` construction stratum** (the `OpParams`/`readonly` state-stratification home, `L4/index.md:24`): `fe_collection` is the basis the space is built over, `fe_space` the construction itself, `essential_dofs` the BC dof-set carried for the separable post-composition — none shape the fold, so none gets a standalone thin chapter (the combinator-as-entry default; re-open per downstream-consumer demand, see §Open questions).
- `terms` — `[WeakFormTerm]` — the immutable, finite list of weak-form contributions the fold ranges over. Each element is a [`weak_form_term`](../L1/weak_form_term.md) — a `(coefficient, differential-operator)` pair (firm cycle-061). The combinator **quantifies over the term list opaquely**: the fold's structure and laws never crack open a term's internals (the `differential-operator` axis — ∇/Gradient, ∇×/Curl, I/mass — is leaf content inside `assemble_term`). The L0 two-list (domain + boundary) structure is one concatenated list here (`domain_integs ++ boundary_integs`); the `term-position` split is the absorbed axis.
- result — `LinearOperator[N, N]` — a fresh global linear operator over the space's true-dof axis `N`, the **sum** of the per-term contributions.
- `assemble_term` — the **opaque per-term assembly leaf**: it takes one weak-form term to its global-dof `LinearOperator[N,N]` contribution (the element-local quadrature kernel + restriction; libCEED-owned). Per [`black-box-vs-accelerated-kernels`](../concepts/black-box-vs-accelerated-kernels.md) case 1, it is a **black-box kernel that rises as a `readonly` opaque-surface input**: clean signature `(space, term) -> LinearOperator[N,N]`, opaque body (the backend supplies it). The combinator is defined by the FOLD over `assemble_term`; `assemble_term` itself is a leaf the fold quantifies over, not part of `fe_assemble`'s definition — the same way [`eigsolve`](./eigsolve.md) hands `apply_shift_invert` to the library and folds over `eigen_iterate`, and [`fold_solve`](./fold_solve.md) folds over `time_step_op`. Its L0 reality is the [`fe-assemble-libceed-boundary-obstruction`](../L1-L0/fe-assemble-libceed-boundary-obstruction.md) `obstruction (opaque-library-ownership)` (`integ->Assemble(...)` building one `CeedOperator` sub-operator, `palace/fem/bilinearform.cpp:75-76`); the L4 black-box-input lifts that obstruction (the positive reframe of the opaque boundary for the backend-lowering purpose).

The shape contract makes three things structural at the combinator level that are merely conventional in the Palace C++ build-up-then-assemble protocol:

1. **The space is captured once, outside the fold.** `space : FiniteElementSpace[N]` appears once in the signature, bound before the `foldr`; every per-term `assemble_term` reads the *same* `space`. This forbids per-term space mutation at the type level (FE assembly never re-spaces mid-fold; the L0 `BilinearForm` holds one fixed `fespace`).
2. **The per-term leaf is opaque; the fold is the algebra.** `assemble_term` is a black-box-kernel input — the combinator's structure and laws are about the LIST and operator-`+`; they never inspect a term's `(coefficient, differential-operator)` internals. The fold lifts (it is the genuinely-new L4 vocabulary); the leaf stays an opaque input (the libCEED boundary).
3. **The reduction is a commutative-monoid sum.** The `foldr` reduces by operator-`+` over `LinearOperator[N,N]` — a commutative, associative monoid with identity `zero`. This is what makes the map-then-reduce a homomorphism (§Algebraic laws law 1) and the terms commute (law 3) — the structural distinction from `fold_solve`, whose `foldl` reduces by a non-commutative carry-threading step.

## Semantics

`fe_assemble space terms` is the complete FE operator assembly expressed as a `foldr` of the black-box leaf `assemble_term` over the term list, **summed** by operator-`+`. It has two equivalent presentations (the §Signature renders both):

1. **Direct `foldr` form** — `fe_assemble space terms = foldr (\t acc -> assemble_term space t + acc) zero terms`. The combinator *is* a fold: the per-term function `assemble_term space` closes over the once-captured `space` and is applied independently to each term, then reduced into the accumulator by operator-`+`. The empty fold is the identity `zero` (the zero operator).

2. **Map-then-reduce (homomorphic) form** — `fe_assemble space terms = sum [ assemble_term space t | t <- terms ]`. Because the reduction monoid (`+`, `zero`) is commutative and associative, the `foldr` is exactly a `map` of `assemble_term space` over the terms followed by a `sum` — a **list homomorphism**. This is the presentation that makes the algebraic laws (§Algebraic laws) immediate, and the one that makes the **sibling relationship to [`solve_family`](./solve_family.md)** structural: `solve_family` is `map (ksp_solve op)` collected to a `[SimState]` list (the reduction is list-concatenation / identity collection); `fe_assemble` is `map (assemble_term space)` reduced to a single `LinearOperator[N,N]` by operator-`+`. Both are concatenation-homomorphisms over independent per-element work; they differ only in the reduction monoid (list-collect vs operator-sum). The choice between the two presentations is a presentation rotation, not a semantic distinction.

The operator's **action** on a vector `v` is the sum of the per-term actions:

    fe_assemble space terms · v  =  Σ_{t ∈ terms} (assemble_term space t · v)

— each weak-form term contributes an independent additive piece to the global operator's action; there is no cross-term coupling. This is the standard conforming-FE assembly model: the global operator is the sum of element-local weak-form contributions, with no inter-element coupling beyond shared-dof assembly (already absorbed inside each `assemble_term`).

The combinator is **pure** at L4 (per the strawman §3.5 operator-application and §3.3–3.4 record/state conventions): there is no built-up container, no sub-operator accumulator mutated in place, no `Finalize()` step. The L0 source constructs a `BilinearForm`, mutates its integrator lists by `push_back`, and mutates a `ceed::Operator` by `AddSubOperator`; all three mutations are L3-and-below concerns reintroduced by the [`fe-assemble-fold-dissolution`](../L4-L3/fe-assemble-fold-dissolution.md) theme. At L4 the relationship is purely the algebraic `foldr`/sum.

Per [`state-stratification`](../concepts/state-stratification.md), `space` is the shared `readonly` construction stratum captured once; the term list is the per-call input; the per-term `CeedOperator` sub-operators are library-internal ephemeral (not even a Palace `let`-bound bundle — they live inside `assemble_term`). The fold introduces no monadic effect — `assemble_term` is a *plain function* the combinator folds, not a `Solve` action (unlike the [`ksp_solve`](./ksp_solve.md) / [`eigsolve`](./eigsolve.md) caps whose drivers thread `SimState` / `EigState`). The combinator is therefore a **pure function** `(space, terms) -> LinearOperator[N,N]` (modulo the floating-point non-associativity of the accumulated numeric matrix entries, which is an L3-and-below representation concern, not an L4 algebraic distinction — the *operator* is order-independent; see law 3).

### Demand-pruning interaction

Under the strawman §3.8 pruning rule (`book/src/design/l4_calculus.md:186-228`), the assembled operator materializes only when a downstream consumer reads it — `fe_assemble space terms` whose result `K` is never applied prunes the whole fold. In Palace every assembled `K` is unconditionally consumed (handed to `SetOperators` / a Krylov solve immediately after assembly), so the operator never prunes in practice; but the combinator's typing makes the demand-driven materialization structural. Within the fold, the per-term `assemble_term space t` contributions all feed the single summed result — there is no per-term observation point a consumer could selectively read, so (unlike `solve_family`'s per-element solutions, which CAN prune individually) the fold prunes all-or-nothing at the operator boundary.

## Algebraic laws

`fe_assemble` is a **list homomorphism (a `foldr` producing a commutative-monoid sum)**, so its laws are exactly the laws of that fold specialised to the FE term list, plus the space-capture-once hoist that licenses them. They hold treating `assemble_term` and `WeakFormTerm` as opaque (the black-box-input discipline). They are the L4-vocabulary statements of the four firm L1 laws (`L1/fe_assemble.md:112-142`), lifted to the combinator. Absences are catalogued explicitly to prevent decoration drift.

1. **Concatenation-homomorphism / term-list additivity** (the load-bearing law — the one that makes this the homomorphic sibling of `solve_family`). `fe_assemble space (terms₁ ++ terms₂) = fe_assemble space terms₁ + fe_assemble space terms₂`. Splitting or concatenating the term list distributes over operator addition — both sides equal `Σ_{t ∈ terms₁} assemble_term space t + Σ_{t ∈ terms₂} assemble_term space t`. This is the fold's defining homomorphism (over the operator-`+` monoid); it is what makes the domain/boundary two-list L0 structure a single L4 fold (`domain_integs` and `boundary_integs` are two sub-lists whose assembled sums add), and it is what licenses chunking, reordering, and the embarrassingly-parallel realization (the term contributions can be assembled in any partition and summed). The L4-vocabulary lift of `L1/fe_assemble.md:125-130` (law 2). It is the **operator-sum analog of `solve_family`'s list-collect concatenation-homomorphism** (`L4/solve_family.md:88`): both fold independent per-element work over a list; `solve_family` reduces by list-concatenation, `fe_assemble` by operator-`+`.

2. **Space-capture-once / construction-hoist** (the identity that makes the combinator worth naming). `fe_assemble space terms = sum (map (assemble_term space) terms)`, and the `space`-dependent construction (the FE-space build + the restriction/basis setup `assemble_term` reads) is **invariant across the fold**, so it hoists out of it (computed once, before the term list is traversed). This is the L4 typing of the single `BilinearForm k(space)` construction shared by all `AddDomainIntegrator` calls (electrostatic `palace/models/laplaceoperator.cpp:193`, magnetostatic `palace/models/curlcurloperator.cpp:180`). **Consequence**: the structural payoff is that the per-term leaf shares the once-built space's restriction/basis — exactly the libCEED `CeedElemRestriction` / `CeedBasis` inputs built once per geometry and reused across the integrator fold (`palace/fem/bilinearform.cpp:64-69` built outside the inner `for (const auto &integ : domain_integs)` loop at `:71`).

3. **Term-position commutativity / order-independence** (the map's naturality + the monoid commutativity). `fe_assemble space terms` is invariant under any permutation of `terms`, because operator addition is commutative and associative — `Σ` does not depend on summation order: `fe_assemble space (permute terms) = fe_assemble space terms` for any permutation. The L0 `push_back` order of `AddDomainIntegrator` calls, and the domain-then-boundary loop order in the L0 fold, are therefore algebraically immaterial. The L4-vocabulary lift of `L1/fe_assemble.md:136-142` (law 4). This is the **load-bearing distinction from [`fold_solve`](./fold_solve.md)**: `fold_solve`'s `foldl` reduces by a carry-threading step where each step's input is the prior step's output, so it does NOT commute (`L4/fold_solve.md` load-bearing non-law); `fe_assemble`'s reduction is a commutative monoid, so it does. (Caveat: floating-point non-associativity of the accumulated numeric matrix entries is an L3-and-below representation concern — the *operator* is order-independent.)

4. **Empty-term identity** (`fe_assemble space [] = zero`). The fold over the empty term list is the fold's identity element (the zero operator on `LinearOperator[N,N]`, the additive identity for operator-`+`). The L4-vocabulary lift of `L1/fe_assemble.md:118-123` (law 1). A degenerate (not algebraic) identity — Palace excludes the empty term list at the source level (a solver always assembles at least one integrator), so the empty case is a calculus-level total-definition convenience, the same flavor as `solve_family`'s empty-family degenerate (`L4/solve_family.md:94`).

5. **Single-term reduction** (`fe_assemble space [t] = assemble_term space t`). A one-term assembly is just that term's contribution — the witness electrostatic case `fe_assemble h1_space [diffusion(ε)]` = the permittivity-weighted diffusion operator (`palace/models/laplaceoperator.cpp:191-194`). Laws that explicitly **do not** hold:

- **No identity / non-degeneracy guarantee on a single term.** `assemble_term space t` need not be invertible, SPD, or nonzero (a pure diffusion operator is singular before BC-elimination — the constant null-space). `fe_assemble` carries no SPD/Hermitian precondition. Inherited from `L1/fe_assemble.md:147-149`.
- **BC-elimination is NOT part of the fold.** Pinning essential (Dirichlet) dofs (`eliminate_essential_bc`) and lifting inhomogeneous Dirichlet data into the RHS (`eliminate_rhs`) are **separable post-compositions** on the assembled operator, valid independently of how it was assembled — not laws of `fe_assemble`. They consume `essential_dofs` (absorbed into the `readonly` construction stratum) but compose *after* the fold. Inherited from `L1/fe_assemble.md:150-155`; they are sibling deferred operators (the rank-3/4 c069 candidates, gated on primitive-L4-presence per the planner OQ).
- **No per-term observability / selective demand-pruning.** Unlike `solve_family`'s per-element solutions (which can prune individually), the per-term contributions all feed the single summed `K`; there is no per-term observation point. The fold prunes all-or-nothing at the operator boundary (§"Demand-pruning interaction").
- **No carry-threading / sequential dependence.** The fold is a commutative-monoid reduce (law 3); it is NOT a state-threaded `fold_solve`-style sequential fold. There is no "prior term's output is the next term's input" dependence — the explicit non-`fold_solve` fact that the map-not-fold mining-gate guard (step 3) turns on.
