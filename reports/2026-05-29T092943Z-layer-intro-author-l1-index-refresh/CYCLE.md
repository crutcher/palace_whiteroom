## Taxonomy reasoning (philosophical-soundness check, task 1)

The existing four motifs partition the firm cohort by **operand/result character**: (1) tensor→tensor element-local, (2) tensor→scalar fold, (3) a structural sibling-relation (not an operand class), (4) opaque-structured-operator → structured-result *absorption to convergence*. The two new operators fit none:

- **`assemble-diagonal`** consumes an **opaque** `LinearOperator[N, N]` and returns **operator-intrinsic data** (`diag(A)`) — it takes *no vector operand*. The entry is explicit (`book/src/L1/assemble-diagonal.md:9`, resolving OQ `assemblediagonal-is-not-apply-linop-variant`) that this is **not** an `apply_linop` variant: there is no `x` to be linear in; the result is a property of `A` alone. Its laws are operator-introspection laws (diagonal-of-scale, diagonal-of-sum, identity-round-trip), not vector-update laws. The natural sibling is `apply_linop` on the *operator/action* side. This is the **operator/data divide**.
- **`lu_solve`** consumes a **dense materialized** `Matrix[k, k]` (it *reads* `A`'s entries) and solves `A x = b` by a direct factorization in small coordinate space. The entry is explicit (`book/src/L1/lu_solve.md:35,69`) that this is **not** an `apply_linop` variant (`apply_linop` never reads its opaque operator's entries) and **not** a `ksp_solve` chain — it is the *small-dense-direct sibling* of `ksp_solve` (large-sparse-iterative), split by the dense-direct vs sparse-iterative representation/cost distinction. Its operands live in **coordinate space** (`k` = deflation rank / ROM basis size, single to low tens), where motifs 1–2 operate on large field-space tensors of dimension `N`. This is the **dense-coordinate-space vs sparse-field-space divide**.

**Why TWO, not one.** A single combined motif would have to be titled by what these operators lack in common with the rest ("operations that don't take a field vector") — a negative grab-bag framing that obscures *two distinct positive organizing axes*. The operator/data divide (introspection) and the dense-coordinate/sparse-field divide (small-dense algebra) are orthogonal: one is about *what you extract from an operator*, the other about *what space and representation you compute in*. Future L1 operators populate each independently — Jacobi / block-Jacobi / spectral-norm extraction grow motif 5 (all operator-introspection); ROM-online / deflation small-dense work grows motif 6 (all coordinate-space dense algebra). Each motif names a positive vocabulary family a reader can use to predict where a new operator lands. Folding them together would merge two predictive axes into one residual bucket. Per the role discipline that motifs are positive vocabulary families (not residual buckets), the split is the sounder taxonomy. The planner's provisional titles are preserved in spirit: motif 5 keeps "operator-to-data introspection"; motif 6 keeps "small-dense direct solve in coordinate space" but generalised to "coordinate-space dense direct algebra" so it can later host the non-solve dense operators (Gram products, dense factorize-and-back-project) that the cycle-022 wave-2 `deflate`/`gram` L2 combinators decompose into.

**Sibling-coverage note (open question, not in scope to fix here):** the split surfaces that **`apply_linop`** has been implicitly riding under motif 4 ("constructed-operator absorption") but is properly an *opaque-operator action* — the operator/action sibling of `assemble-diagonal`'s operator/data introspection. Motif 4 is specifically *absorption-to-convergence/structured-result* (`ksp_solve`, `eigsolve`, `chebyshev-smoother`, `divfree-projector` — operators whose constructed argument is solved/iterated/applied-as-policy). A pure single opaque-operator *action* (`apply_linop`) is a thinner thing. I name this in motif 5's text (the operator/action sibling) but do not re-file `apply_linop` — see Open questions.
